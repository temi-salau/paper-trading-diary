## Week 31 Reflection — 2026-07-29 to 2026-08-02

# Summary:
- Both tracks entered AAPL on 2026-07-29:
    - Discretionary bought 10 shares @ £340.19
    - Systematic bought 5 shares @ £341.03, triggered by a bullish MA crossover from 07-08.
- AAPL reported record Q3 2026 results on 07-31 (revenue $109.4B +16% YoY, EPS $2.02 +29% YoY), yet sold off ~9.4% through the week, suggesting the decline was driven more by the AI-bug/reputational issue than the earnings print itself.
- Noted a data discrepancy between platforms mid-week (Investopedia £333.43 vs Alpaca £324.99 on 07-30), which had converged by week close (both £308.91 on 08-02), consistent with Alpaca's IEX feed lagging Investopedia's broader quote during a volatile period.

# What worked / what didn't:
**Discretionary:**
- The initial reasoning (AAPL as a liquid, stable entry point based on historical trend) was sound given the information available at the time, this wasn't a bad decision in hindsight, it was a reasonable read of months of price history.
- What it exposed: months of historical trend data doesn't meaningfully predict outcomes over a 2-3 day horizon, especially around a binary catalyst like earnings. Judging safety on a long lookback window while entering right before a short-term event is a mismatch in timeframes, not necessarily a flawed thesis.
- Lesson: when using historical trend as justification, be explicit about which timeframe the reasoning actually applies to, long-term stability doesn't protect against short-term, event-driven moves.

**Systematic:**
- Not automating first was the right call for trade one; entering manually off the signal output let me sanity check the logic before trusting a script to execute unsupervised.
- The 10/20-day rolling window structure was a reasonable way to visualise trend, but the specific windows chosen (10 and 20 days) are too short-horizon-sensitive for a strategy trading on a stock with the day-to-day and intraday volatility AAPL showed this week.
- Recognised this risk going in, position sizing (5 shares, ~1.7% of portfolio) was kept conservative specifically because I knew the signal wasn't fully reliable on its own, not overcommitting to an unproven approach.
- The signal alone isn't enough going forward, worth exploring additional signals rather than relying purely on MA crossover.

# Comparison (systematic vs discretionary):
**Similarities:**
- Both entered AAPL within a day of each other, both are down roughly the same % (~9.4%) by week close, the stock-specific move dominated over any difference in approach this week.

**Differences in process:**
- Discretionary reasoning explicitly weighed a known, specific risk (earnings) before entering; systematic entered purely off a mechanical signal with no awareness of the earnings date at all, the model has no concept of an upcoming catalyst.
- When new information emerged mid-week (the AI-bugs issue, the earnings beat itself), discretionary could theoretically incorporate that going forward; systematic has no mechanism to react to news at all, it can only react once price movement is large enough to actually shift the moving averages, which this week, it didn't.
- This is the core structural difference exposed this week: discretionary can reason about *why* something is happening, systematic can only react to *that* something happened, and even then with a lag.

**Open question:**
- Does discretionary's information advantage this week actually translate into better decisions, or does it just feel that way? Both positions are down almost identically, so "knowing the reason" didn't yet change the outcome, worth watching whether that gap
  appears over a longer stretch.

# Adjustments for next week:

- Discretionary: read more into company fundamentals/news before entering, not just historical price trend, to avoid the timeframe mismatch seen this week (long lookback used to justify a short-term entry).
- Discretionary: check the earnings/catalyst calendar before every entry as a standing process step, so timing risk is flagged deliberately rather than noticed after the fact.
