**Discretionary:**

- The NVDA thesis itself (AI/data-center demand plus personal domain knowledge from gaming) was reasoned through carefully, including catching and correcting a conflation between NVDA's gaming revenue and its larger AI/data-center revenue.
- The planned entry did not happen this week due to being unwell. A legitimate reason, but it does mean the decision-making test, whether this thesis holds up once real capital and timing are involved, is still untested going into next week.

## Comparison (systematic vs discretionary):
**Similarities:**
- Neither track took real action on a position this week. Systematic's output stayed unchanged ("hold, loss protection") throughout, and discretionary's planned NVDA entry didn't execute. Both weeks are better described by what was built/reasoned through than by what was traded.

**Differences in process:**
- Systematic's inaction was purely a function of the rules themselves, the loss-protection gate held regardless of anything else happening underneath it, an automated, consistent outcome.
- Discretionary's inaction was circumstantial, not rule-driven, the plan existed and was reasoned through, but execution was interrupted.

**Open question:**
- Systematic doesn't get tired, doesn't have an off week, its "hold" this week was fully determined by data. Discretionary's "no action" this week had nothing to do with the market and everything to do with real life. Important to think about what that asymmetry actually means for a real trading operation: systematic scales and stays consistent regardless of personal circumstances, but it also can't adapt or apply judgment the way a person can when something genuinely new shows up. Neither is better, they're just different kinds of reliability.
