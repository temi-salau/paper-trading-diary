## What worked / what didn't:
**Discretionary:**
- Entering with a strong thesis before the earnings report, rather than waiting to react afterward, was the right call, being positioned ahead of the event is what let the confirmation actually mean something to the process, rather than chasing the move after the fact.
- Watching how the market reacted to earnings, not just the earnings themselves, was the right instinct, the reaction (breaking NVDA's four-quarter pattern) turned out to be more informative than the headline numbers alone.
- Genuinely don't have enough of a read on NVDA yet, only one week in, one data point isn't enough to judge the thesis properly, this needs more time before drawing real conclusions.
- Worth flagging: the sharp post-earnings gain (08-27, +1.00%, actually a bigger jump intraday given the after-hours spike) was followed by an almost equally sharp pullback the very next day (08-28, -3.83%). Not necessarily a bad sign, but worth watching whether this becomes a pattern (spike then give-back) or settles into a genuine new range.

**Systematic:**
- Multi-stock support was built and genuinely verified, not just assumed working, tested via a throwaway file with fake multi-symbol position data (AAPL and NVDA both processed correctly, distinct outputs for each), confirming the loop structure itself is sound.
- The real limitation right now isn't the code, it's the data: Alpaca still only holds one live position (AAPL), so despite building genuine multi-stock capability, the system has never actually run the loop for real against more than one symbol.
- The loss-protection override and RSI-downgrade logic, flagged as untested in prior weeks, remain untested this week too, AAPL's signal conditions still haven't produced the scenario needed to trigger either one.

## Comparison (systematic vs discretionary):
**Similarities:**
- Both tracks are, in different ways, waiting on more data before drawing real conclusions. Discretionary needs more time with NVDA specifically (one week isn't enough). Systematic needs a second real Alpaca position before its multi-stock capability actually gets exercised.

**Differences in process:**
- Discretionary had a genuinely eventful week, a real thesis met a real event (NVDA earnings) and produced a real, informative outcome, the pattern-break and the following pullback. Systematic's week was almost entirely architectural, real, meaningful progress, but with zero change in live output or behaviour, still just tracking one stock, still stuck on "hold."
- Discretionary's limitation this week was about time (only one week of data on NVDA) whilst systematic's limitation is about *scope* (only one real position to work with, regardless of how much time passes), is a different kind of constraint that won't resolve on its own the way discretionary's will.

**Open question:**
- Discretionary's open question from last week (does NVDA's earnings reaction hold or fade) partially resolved, it held, but a new one replaced it, is the spike-then-pullback pattern a one-off or something recurring. Systematic's open questions haven't moved at all, the same untested rules from Week 32 are still untested in Week 35. Worth considering whether that's simply bad luck (AAPL hasn't produced the right conditions) or whether the systematic side needs a genuinely different, more active approach to actually get tested, rather than passively waiting for the right day to arrive.
